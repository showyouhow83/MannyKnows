## Two different things get called "a dental website"

The first is the **build**. Someone designs the site, places the copy, codes it, launches it, and hands it over.

The second is the **built-and-run site**. The same build, plus everything after launch, paid monthly. Hosting, security, speed, the new hygienist's bio, the updated insurance list, and the search work that keeps you showing up when someone in Chicopee types "dentist near me."

A practice website is never done. Providers join and leave, you add clear aligners or drop a PPO, Google changes. That is why most numbers below come in two parts, upfront and monthly, and why the useful comparison is the first-year total, not the sticker.

## What actually drives the price of a practice site

Scope. These items separate a $600 template from a $12,000 proposal.

- **Procedure and service pages.** A page per procedure (implants, crowns, clear aligners, pediatric visits) is what ranks and what a nervous patient reads before calling. Ten real pages cost more than one "Services" list.
- **Photography of the real office.** Stock smiles look like every competitor. Photos of your operatories and team cost a half-day shoot and are the fastest trust signal on the site.
- **New-patient forms.** Downloadable PDFs are cheap. Forms a patient completes on a phone before the visit take real work and a secure way to receive them.
- **Insurance and financing content.** Which plans you take, what out-of-network means, whether you offer a membership plan. Unglamorous, and among the most-read pages on a practice site.
- **Booking.** A contact form is not booking. Real booking means an appointment lands in the schedule, at an open time, without a phone call.
- **Local SEO, per location.** One office is one Google Business Profile and one set of location pages. Three offices triples the work.
- **Upkeep.** Who changes the hours, adds the associate, and fixes the broken form. If the answer is "you, when you remember," the site is cheaper on paper and more expensive in practice.

## What the market charges for dental website design in 2026

Published ranges, with who published them; where the source sells websites itself, we say so.

**DIY builders.** [The Dental Signal's 2026 cost guide](https://www.thedentalsignal.com/dental-practice-website-cost-2026/) puts a template or DIY dental site at $500 to $1,500 to set up and $20 to $50 a month after. You do the writing, the photos, the forms, and the SEO.

**Freelancers and agencies.** The same guide puts a professionally built dental site between $3,000 and $15,000, with maintenance at $100 to $500 a month, online scheduling $200 to $400 a month as an add-on, and professional photography $500 to $1,500. [Rosemont Media](https://www.rosemontmedia.com/website-design/how-much-should-a-dental-website-cost/), a dental and medical marketing agency (so this is a vendor's number), quotes custom dental sites at $3,500 to $30,000 and more, recommends a new practice budget $10,000 to $16,000, and suggests $1,000 to $2,000 a month for ongoing content.

**Dental-specific subscription vendors.** These sell a templated practice site for a monthly fee plus setup. [ProSites' own pricing page](https://www.prosites.com/pricing/) names four tiers but publishes no dollar figures; you request a demo. The published estimates come from competitors. [FlashCrafter's comparison](https://www.flashcrafter.ai/dental-marketing-growth-engine/website-cost) (also a vendor competing for the same practices) puts ProSites at $299 to $599 a month plus $500 to $1,500 setup, and Officite at $349 to $699 a month plus $1,000 to $2,000 setup. Treat those as ballpark and get the vendor's number in writing.

Do the year-one arithmetic on The Dental Signal's figures alone and a professionally built practice site with basic maintenance runs $4,200 to $21,000 in the first twelve months, before scheduling or photography. That is the number to compare any monthly plan against.

## The expensive website

It's the cheap one. A $40-a-month template that never ranks for a single procedure in your city, loads slowly on a phone, and ends in a contact form nobody checks costs you every new patient it fails to catch. One implant case a year pays for a real site several times over. The question isn't "what's the lowest price," it's "what does the site have to bring in to pay for itself," and for a practice that number is small.

## What to ask any vendor before you sign

1. **Who owns the domain and the content?** You should, in your name, from day one. If the vendor registers the domain, get it transferred before launch.
2. **Who does the upkeep, and what does it cost?** Get the hourly rate or monthly scope in writing. "Just email us" without a price is a bill you haven't seen yet.
3. **Is SEO included, or a separate contract?** Many practice-site vendors sell the site and the search work as two products. Ask what "included" covers.
4. **Is booking real, or is it a form?** Ask to see an appointment land in a schedule. If the patient "requests a time and you call them back," that's a form.
5. **What happens if we leave?** What you keep, what stops, and whether there's a fee, in writing, before you're a customer.
