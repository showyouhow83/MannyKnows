A patient with a cracked molar in Chicopee doesn't browse. She types "dentist near me," sees three practices pinned on a map, and calls one. The parent searching "pediatrician near me" in Westfield and the person with a fever typing "urgent care near me" in Holyoke do the same. If your practice isn't one of those three, or at least on the first screen under them, that patient books somewhere else.

This is what it takes to be the practice they pick, and how the answer changes from town to town in Western Mass.

## What Google shows for "dentist near me"

At the top of the page sits the **map pack**, three Google Business Profiles with a star rating, review count, hours, and Call, Directions, and Website buttons. Under it come the organic results, and for many health searches an AI Overview now sits above both. Backlinko's local search research found [42% of local searchers click a result inside the map pack](https://backlinko.com/local-seo-stats), so three profiles collect close to half the clicks.

Google says the pack is ranked on [relevance, distance, and prominence](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091). Distance you can't change. Relevance and prominence are the job, and Google says that "businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up" and that "more reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking."

The map pack ranks your **profile**; the organic results rank your **website**. A practice that wants both slots builds both.

## The Google Business Profile, done the way a practice needs it

The [general profile walkthrough](/blog/google-business-profile-springfield/) covers claiming and verifying. Practices need a few things beyond that.

- **Primary category.** "Dentist" and "Dental clinic" rank differently, and so do "Doctor," "Family practice physician," "Pediatrician," and "Urgent care center." Pick the one that matches what patients search for, then add secondaries such as "Cosmetic dentist," "Emergency dental service," or "Pediatric dentist." Practitioner profiles are allowed too; keep them consistent with the practice profile so they don't compete with it.
- **Services, listed one by one.** Implants, Invisalign, root canals, same-day crowns, sports physicals, telehealth visits. Each one is a phrase Google can match a search to.
- **Hours, including the ones that matter most.** If you take emergency or same-day patients, say so in the hours and the description. A wrong "closed" costs you the patient who is ready to call now.
- **Real photos of the office and the team.** The front door, the parking lot, the waiting room, an operatory, the team. A profile that adds photos every month reads as open and busy, and a 360° photo lets a patient step inside from their phone.
- **Q&A, seeded by you.** Do you take MassHealth? Is there parking? Do you see children? Answer what patients ask at the front desk before a stranger answers for you.
- **A booking link and insurance in the description.** The appointment link goes in its own field; the plans you accept and the languages your staff speak go in the description.

Then post every week or two, a new provider, a hygiene tip, a holiday schedule, so the profile never looks abandoned. The [profile section of our local SEO page](/local-seo/#gbp) shows what we keep current each month.

## Reviews, recency, and replies

Reviews decide the map pack and the click. BrightLocal's 2026 [Local Consumer Review Survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/) found that 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses, 74% want reviews written in the last three months, and 47% won't use a business with fewer than twenty. Replies matter almost as much, with 80% saying they're inclined to use a business that answers all of its reviews.

Volume comes from asking every patient, every visit, in a way that takes one tap. A same-day text with the direct link beats a card at checkout, and the same survey found 83% of people who were asked went on to leave one. Reply to every review, good and bad, without confirming that the person is a patient and without any health detail.

Two things you never do. Don't offer anything for a review, and don't screen patients so only happy ones get the link. Google's Business Profile policy [prohibits incentives](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122) of any kind and separately forbids practices that "discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews," per its [prohibited content rules](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114). Our [review guide](/blog/get-more-google-reviews/) covers the ask itself in detail.

## The website side of "dentist near me"

Your profile earns the pack; your website earns the organic slots and closes the patient who clicks through.

1. **A page per procedure or service.** "Dental Implants in Springfield, MA" ranks and converts; a bullet on a generic services page does neither. Medical practices do the same for physicals, same-day sick visits, and telehealth.
2. **A page per location** for practices with more than one office, each with its own address, hours, providers, photos, and directions.
3. **City and service in the title tag**, written for a person, not stuffed for a robot.
4. **Schema markup.** Dentist, MedicalBusiness, Physician, or MedicalClinic structured data with the address, phone, hours, and geo coordinates that match the profile exactly. The same name, address, and phone everywhere, from your site to Healthgrades and the insurer directories, is what Google checks when deciding how much to trust you.
5. **Speed, mobile, and accessibility.** Most of these searches happen on a phone in the moment of need. A slow page loses the patient, and a page that fails with a screen reader or large text loses the older patients every practice depends on.

## The AI-answer layer

A growing share of "near me" now starts inside an assistant. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 45% of consumers have used ChatGPT or similar tools for local business recommendations, up from 6% the year before. On Google itself, a [Pew Research study](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) from 2025 found that when an AI summary appears, users click a traditional result on 8% of visits versus 15% when there is none. When the answer is written for the patient, being the practice cited inside it is what's left to win.

Nobody outside Google or OpenAI can promise that placement. What we can say is that these systems draw on the same public facts as the map pack. Specific pages that answer a real question directly (do you take this insurance, do you see children, what does a first visit involve), the same details on every directory, and clean structured data give an assistant something accurate to quote. ## In Western Mass specifically

"Dentist near me" is a different search in Springfield than in Northampton, because distance is one of Google's three factors and the pack is drawn around the searcher, not around you. A practice on Sumner Avenue can own the Forest Park pack and be invisible from Holyoke.

That is why one generic page never covers a service area. If you take patients from Chicopee, Agawam, and Westfield, each town needs its own page with real content, which providers see patients from there, how long the drive is, which local plans you take, and the profile has to match. A second office in Holyoke or Northampton needs a second profile at that address, never a mailbox or a virtual office, which Google's guidelines don't allow. Multi-town ranking is a page and profile strategy per town, and it compounds over months, not weeks.

## What to measure every month

- Map-pack position for your main terms in each town you serve, not just from your own front desk.
- Calls, direction requests, and website clicks from the profile's performance report.
- Bookings that came through the site and the profile link.
- Review count, average rating, and how many reviews arrived in the last ninety days.
- New-patient calls that mention Google when the front desk asks.

If those five move together, the work is working. If calls climb but bookings don't, the problem moved to the phone, which is a different fix.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to rank for "dentist near me"?

Lightly contested towns can move within a couple of months once the profile is complete and reviews are arriving. Springfield proper, where dozens of practices compete, takes longer, and the pack keeps shifting.

### Do we need a separate page for every town we serve?

Yes, if you want to show up there. Google ranks the pack by the searcher's location, so a Springfield practice needs a real page for Chicopee patients and another for Agawam, each with content specific to that town rather than one paragraph with the name swapped.

### Can you do this on the website we already have?

Yes. The local SEO packages work on any site, including ones we didn't build. If the site is too slow or too old to carry the pages and schema, we'll say so and show you the options.

### Do reviews really affect map ranking?

Yes. Google's own [local ranking help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking, and BrightLocal's 2026 survey shows patients screen on volume and recency before they call.

### Is buying links or reviews ever worth it?

Never. Paid reviews violate [Google's review policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) and paid links violate its [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies); either can lower or remove your rankings. The pack is earned with reviews, photos, and pages.
