It's 8:40 on a Tuesday night. A mother in Chicopee has a kid with a chipped tooth, your website is open on her phone, and all she wants to know is whether you take her insurance and when someone can see him. Your front desk went home at five. She reads what she can, goes back to Google, and taps the next practice. Nobody at your office will ever know she was there.

That is the gap an AI agent on a practice website closes. This post covers what patients ask and when, what an agent should and should not do for a dental or medical office, how booking works, how the agent is trained, what happens to patient information, and what changes for the people at your desk.

## What patients actually ask, and when they ask it

Any front desk hears the same questions on repeat:

- "Do you take my insurance?" and "are you in network with my plan?"
- "Are you accepting new patients?"
- "What are your hours?" and "are you open Saturday?"
- "How much is a cleaning, a crown, a new-patient exam, a physical without insurance?"
- "Where do I park, and which entrance?"
- "Do you see kids?" and "do you do that procedure?"
- "It's an emergency, what do I do?"
- "Can I get in this week?"

None of those need a clinician. Most don't need a human at all. They need an accurate answer, right now, from something that knows the practice. And a large share of them arrive when nobody is there to give one. Zocdoc reports that [roughly half of the appointments booked through it are booked between 5 p.m. and 9 a.m.](https://www.zocdoc.com/resources/blog/article/why-not-offering-after-hours-booking-is-driving-away-patients/), when offices are closed, and that [about one in five is booked on a Saturday](https://www.zocdoc.com/resources/blog/article/meet-your-potential-patients-12-facts-about-zocdoc-users/). In Experian Health's 2024 patient-access survey, [89% of patients said being able to schedule any time through online or mobile tools is important to them](https://www.experian.com/blogs/healthcare/the-state-of-patient-access-2024/).

Your phone line, your voicemail, and your contact form do not answer at 8:40 p.m. An agent does.

## What an AI agent on a practice website does well, and what it should never do

An AI agent for a medical practice is a front-desk assistant that lives on your website. Trained on your practice, it does three things:

- **Answers.** Insurance accepted, hours, new-patient status, services, the prices you choose to publish, directions, what to bring to a first visit, in the patient's own language.
- **Captures the lead.** Name, phone or email, what they need. Emailed to the office as it happens and waiting in the admin in the morning.
- **Books.** On the tiers that include booking, it offers open slots and puts the appointment on the calendar while the patient is still on the page.

Just as important is what it does not do, and we build that fence on purpose:

- **No medical advice.** "Should this tooth hurt this much?" gets "that's a question for the doctor, let's get you an appointment," not a diagnosis.
- **No triage.** If someone describes an emergency, the agent gives the instruction the practice sets at setup (call 911, call the on-call line, go to the ER) and stops there. It does not decide how serious it is.
- **No clinical intake.** Medical history, medications, symptoms, and consent forms stay where they belong, in the practice's own patient portal or on paper at the desk. The agent can point a new patient to the portal. It does not collect that information itself.

A chatbot that tries to be a nurse is a liability. A front-desk assistant that knows its limits is the whole point.

## How booking works

With booking turned on, the agent asks what the patient wants to book (a cleaning, a new-patient exam, a consult), offers open times from the practice's admin calendar, and confirms the slot. That admin calendar is synced with the calendar the practice already runs, so an appointment booked at 8:40 p.m. is on the schedule when the office opens, not sitting in an inbox. The office confirms with the patient the same way it does a phone booking, and if new-patient slots are held back or one provider books differently, those rules are set in the admin.

Zocdoc's 2023 year-end data found that [for every 24 hours between booking and the appointment, the odds of a cancellation grew by 4 percent](https://www.techtarget.com/patientengagement/news/366584263/Zocdoc-Sheds-Light-on-User-Appointment-Scheduling-Trends). A slot held tonight instead of "we'll call you back tomorrow" is a real difference in who shows up.

## How the agent is trained on your practice

The agent is only as good as what it knows, so training is the setup. We call the knowledge base the **Brand Brain**. It starts by scanning the practice's website, then we fill in what a website usually leaves out:

- The full list of accepted insurance plans, and the wording you use for out-of-network patients
- Services and providers, and which provider does what
- Hours, holidays, parking, entrances, what to bring
- New-patient policy, cancellation policy, payment options
- The answers to the questions the desk is tired of repeating
- Tone. Warm and simple for a family dental office, measured for a specialty clinic.
- What it refuses to answer, and how it says so

The practice approves every fact the Brand Brain keeps before the agent goes live. After launch, on the Remi AI tier and up, the office can test conversations in the admin and correct an answer the moment it's wrong; on Lite we make those corrections for you at setup and on request. When the insurance list changes in January, the desk updates it in a minute.

## What about patient privacy?

Here is what we can stand behind.

Remi AI asks only for what a receptionist would ask to reach the person back and hold a time: a name, a phone number or email, and what they'd like to book. It is not a medical record and it is not set up to collect health details. What the practice receives is the lead, the same details a person at the desk would have written on a message pad, and the booking if there is one.

And the boundary. This is a front-desk assistant, not a clinical system. Clinical questions, medical history, and intake forms belong inside the practice's patient portal, and we configure the agent to send them there. If the practice's counsel wants a Business Associate Agreement or has questions about HIPAA, that is a conversation to have with us before setup, not after. We will tell you where the agent fits and where it does not. We will not tell you what the law requires of your practice.

## What it changes for the front desk

Nobody at the desk gets replaced. What changes is what the desk spends its day on.

- **Fewer repeated calls.** Insurance, hours, and parking get answered on the site before they become a call. The calls that still come in need a person.
- **No lead lost overnight.** The 8:40 p.m. mother is a captured lead with a phone number, or a booked appointment, by the time the desk opens. Leads and bookings arrive as they happen, by email and in the admin.
- **A schedule that fills while nobody is there.** A Friday cancellation gets backfilled on Sunday afternoon by the patient who wanted an earlier slot.

## FAQ

### Will patients know they're talking to an AI?

Yes. It identifies itself as an AI assistant, and on the Remi AI tier and up the practice picks its name, greeting, and tone in the admin (on Lite we set them at setup). A patient who wants a person can ask for one, and the conversation is passed to the desk as a lead.

### Can it book directly into our schedule?

Yes, on the tiers with booking, which are Remi AI Pro standalone or the Get Booked website plan. It books into the practice's admin calendar, which is synced to the calendar the office already uses, and the office confirms with the patient.

### What happens if someone describes an emergency?

The agent gives the emergency instruction the practice configures, usually "call 911" or the on-call number, and does not assess how serious it is. It doesn't triage.

### Does it speak Spanish?

Yes. English and Spanish are standard, and it answers in the language the patient starts in. Other languages work the same way.
