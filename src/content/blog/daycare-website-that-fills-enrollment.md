A parent hears your name from a neighbor, or types "daycare near me" into her phone after bedtime, and the next thing she does is look for your website. If it answers her questions, she books a tour. If it's a logo, a phone number, and a stock photo, she moves on to the next center. Here is what a daycare website has to do to fill enrollment, and what we built for a Springfield daycare that had none.

## How parents actually choose a daycare

Word of mouth starts most searches, and the internet finishes them. In the 2019 National Survey of Early Care and Education, the most common way families searched for care combined friends and family with an internet search (37%), and another 28% leaned mainly on internet search and social media ([ACF/OPRE analysis, 2024](https://acf.gov/opre/report/exploring-child-care-and-early-education-search-types-evidence-2019-national-survey)). Parents also compare. In the 2012 survey, 62% of households considered more than one provider ([Urban Institute review, 2024](https://www.urban.org/sites/default/files/2024-05/Parental%20Search%20and%20Selection%20of%20Child%20Care%20and%20Early%20Education-%20A%20Literature%20Review.pdf)).

So the referral gets you on the list, and **the website decides whether you stay on it**. That comparison happens at 10 p.m. after the kids are asleep, when nobody at your center can pick up the phone. Her questions never change. Do you have an opening for a two-year-old, what are your hours, what does it cost, are you licensed, what does a day look like. A site that answers those before the call gets the call.

## The trust signals a daycare website needs

Parents choose with their hearts and their research, so the site has to satisfy both.

- **Real photos of your rooms and your staff.** Parents are picturing their child in that room with that teacher.
- **Licensing and safety.** Massachusetts parents can look up any licensed program in the state's [EEC child care search](https://www.mass.gov/guides/find-a-licensed-family-group-or-school-age-child-care-program), so put your license, ratios, and pickup policy up front.
- **A sample day, the curriculum, and meals.** A schedule tells a parent more than a paragraph of adjectives.
- **Hours, a tuition range, and openings by age group.** A range beats silence, and "now enrolling toddlers" beats "contact us."
- **Enrollment steps** and what happens after the tour.
- **Spanish where your families speak it.** Many Western Mass parents are more comfortable in Spanish, and a site written for them is the difference between a tour booked and a tab closed.

## Ranking in your town

Three things get a daycare found in Springfield, Chicopee, or Holyoke. A complete Google Business Profile with the right primary category ("Day care center" or "Preschool"), real photos, and current hours. Reviews from current families, answered every time; in a 2016 national survey, 27% of parents called ratings on a website "very important" when choosing care ([Urban Institute review](https://www.urban.org/sites/default/files/2024-05/Parental%20Search%20and%20Selection%20of%20Child%20Care%20and%20Early%20Education-%20A%20Literature%20Review.pdf)), and our guide to [getting more Google reviews](/blog/get-more-google-reviews/) applies here too. And one page per program or age group, so "infant care in Chicopee" has a real page to rank, the on-page half of [local SEO](/local-seo/).
